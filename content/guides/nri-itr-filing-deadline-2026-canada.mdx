## The Clock Is Ticking: NRI ITR Deadlines You Cannot Miss

If you are a Canadian NRI with any financial ties to India — a bank account earning interest, a flat generating rental income, mutual fund investments, or a property you sold — the Indian Income Tax Department expects you to file an Income Tax Return (ITR). The deadlines are firm, the penalties for missing them are real, and the process is more complex for NRIs than for resident Indians.

This guide covers everything you need to know about NRI ITR filing for the Assessment Year 2026-27 (Financial Year 2025-26): the critical dates, whether you actually need to file, which ITR form to use, the step-by-step filing process, how to leverage the India-Canada Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement (DTAA), and the mistakes that cost NRIs thousands of rupees every year.

## Key ITR Dates for NRIs in 2026

| Deadline | Date | What It Means |
|---|---|---|
| **End of Financial Year** | March 31, 2026 | Last day of FY 2025-26. All income earned up to this date falls in this assessment year. Also the deadline for tax-saving investments under Section 80C, 80D, etc. |
| **ITR Filing Deadline** | July 31, 2026 | The most important date. This is the last day to file your ITR for FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27) without incurring a late fee. File before this date. |
| **Belated Return Deadline** | December 31, 2026 | If you miss July 31, you can still file a belated return — but with a late fee of up to ₹5,000 under Section 234F, plus interest on any unpaid tax. |
| **Revised Return Deadline** | December 31, 2026 | If you filed on time but made errors, you can file a revised return by this date to correct mistakes. |
| **Tax Audit Report Deadline** | October 31, 2026 | Relevant only if you have business income in India requiring a tax audit under Section 44AB. Most salaried NRIs can ignore this. |
| **Advance Tax Deadlines** | June 15, Sept 15, Dec 15, Mar 15 | If your total tax liability exceeds ₹10,000 in a financial year and TDS does not cover it, you may need to pay advance tax in quarterly installments. |

**The bottom line:** For most Canadian NRIs, **July 31, 2026** is the date that matters. Mark it, plan for it, and file well before it.

## Do You Actually Need to File an ITR?

Not every NRI is required to file. But many NRIs who are not required to file should file anyway. Here is how to determine where you stand.

### You Must File If:

- Your total **Indian income exceeds ₹2.5 lakh** (the basic exemption limit for FY 2025-26) — this includes salary from an Indian employer, rental income, capital gains, FD interest in NRO accounts, and any other Indian-source income
- **TDS was deducted** on your Indian income and you want to claim a **refund** — banks deduct TDS at 30% on NRO FD interest, which may be more than your actual liability
- You **sold property, shares, or mutual funds** in India and need to report capital gains
- You have **business or professional income** in India
- You are transitioning from **NRI to RNOR (Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident)** status

### You Are Not Required to File If:

- Your only Indian income is **NRE FD interest**, which is fully tax-exempt
- Your total Indian income is **below ₹2.5 lakh** and no TDS was deducted

### Why You Should File Even When Not Required

Filing a nil return or a return claiming TDS refund is smart practice, even when not mandatory. It creates a clean compliance record with the Income Tax Department, which matters if you plan to return to India, apply for a home loan, or sell property in the future. It also starts the clock on the assessment window — the department generally cannot reopen your case after a certain period once you have filed.

## Which ITR Form Should Canadian NRIs Use?

This is one of the most common sources of errors. Filing with the wrong form triggers a defective return notice under Section 139(9), which means you have to refile within 15 days — delaying your refund and adding stress.

| ITR Form | Who Should Use It | Common NRI Income Types |
|---|---|---|
| **ITR-1 (Sahaj)** | Residents only — NRIs **cannot** use this form | N/A for NRIs |
| **ITR-2** | NRIs with income from salary, house property, capital gains, FD interest, dividends, and other sources | Rental income, NRO FD interest, mutual fund/stock capital gains, salary from Indian employer |
| **ITR-3** | NRIs with business or professional income in India | Freelance income, consulting fees, business profits from an Indian partnership or proprietorship |
| **ITR-4 (Sugam)** | Residents under presumptive taxation — NRIs generally **cannot** use this | N/A for most NRIs |

**For the vast majority of Canadian NRIs, ITR-2 is the correct form.** It covers all common NRI income scenarios: house property income, capital gains from shares and mutual funds, bank interest, dividends, and salary. Only use ITR-3 if you have business or professional income in India.

### Step 1: Gather Your Documents

Before you begin, collect everything you will need:

**Identity and Residency:**
- PAN card
- Valid passport (all pages with stamps may be needed to determine residential status)
- Canadian visa, PR card, or citizenship certificate
- Travel dates — exact dates of entry and exit from India during FY 2025-26

**Income Documents:**
- Form 16 (if you had Indian salary income)
- Bank statements for all NRE and NRO accounts (April 2025 – March 2026)
- TDS certificates (Form 16A) from banks and tenants
- Capital gains statements from brokers and mutual fund houses
- Rental income details and receipts

**For DTAA Claims:**
- Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) from the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA)
- Form 10F (self-declaration of details not in the TRC)
- Canadian T4 slip and Notice of Assessment

### Step 2: Determine Your Residential Status

Your residential status under the Income Tax Act determines which income is taxable in India. For most Canadian NRIs, the status is straightforward — if you were in India for fewer than 182 days during FY 2025-26 (or fewer than 60 days if you are an Indian citizen with Indian income exceeding ₹15 lakh), you are a Non-Resident.

If you are returning to India or spent significant time there, you may qualify as RNOR — a transitional status that offers some protection from global income taxation. Determining RNOR status is complex and is best handled by a CA.

### Step 3: Log In to the Income Tax Portal

Visit [incometax.gov.in](https://www.incometax.gov.in) and log in with your PAN. If this is your first time, register using your PAN number, Aadhaar (if linked), and a valid email and phone number. Canadian phone numbers are accepted for OTP verification.

### Step 4: Download Form 26AS and AIS

**Form 26AS** is your tax credit statement — it shows all TDS deducted on your behalf by banks, employers, and property buyers. **Annual Information Statement (AIS)** provides a more comprehensive view of your financial transactions in India. Compare both against your own records to ensure nothing is missing or incorrectly reported.

### Step 5: Fill in the ITR Form

Select ITR-2 (or ITR-3 if applicable). Key schedules to complete:

- **Personal Information** — Select "Non-Resident" as your residential status
- **Schedule HP** — For house property income (rental income minus 30% standard deduction, minus home loan interest if applicable)
- **Schedule CG** — For capital gains from property, shares, or mutual fund sales
- **Schedule OS** — For other sources like NRO FD interest
- **Schedule FSI** — For foreign source income (relevant if claiming DTAA benefits)
- **Schedule TR** — For tax relief claimed under DTAA
- **Schedule FA** — For foreign assets (primarily relevant for RNOR, but worth understanding)

### Step 6: File Form 67 for DTAA Benefits

This is the step most NRIs miss — and it costs them dearly. If you are claiming tax relief under the India-Canada DTAA, you **must** file Form 67 before or along with your ITR. Form 67 requires you to upload your Tax Residency Certificate from CRA and declare the details of foreign income and taxes paid.

Without Form 67, your DTAA claim will be denied, and you lose the treaty benefit entirely.

### Step 7: Verify the Tax Computation and Submit

Review the auto-calculated tax liability. Check that all TDS credits from Form 26AS are reflected. If everything looks correct, submit the return.

### Step 8: E-Verify Your Return

You must e-verify your ITR within 30 days of filing. Options include:

- **Aadhaar OTP** — Only if your Aadhaar is linked to your PAN and mobile
- **Net banking** — Through your Indian bank's internet banking portal
- **Digital signature certificate (DSC)** — If you have one
- **Physical verification** — Send a signed ITR-V to CPC Bangalore by post (slowest option)

For NRIs in Canada, net banking is usually the most reliable e-verification method.

## DTAA Benefits: How to Avoid Paying Tax Twice

The India-Canada DTAA is one of the most valuable tools available to Canadian NRIs. Without it, you could end up paying tax on the same income in both countries. Here is how the treaty works in practice.

### What the DTAA Covers

The treaty specifies which country has the right to tax specific types of income, and provides relief mechanisms when both countries tax the same income:

| Income Type | Taxed in India? | Taxed in Canada? | DTAA Relief |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NRO FD interest** | Yes — TDS at 30% (reducible to 15% with TRC) | Yes — worldwide income | Foreign tax credit in Canada for TDS paid in India |
| **NRE FD interest** | No — fully exempt | Yes — worldwide income | No double taxation (only taxed in Canada) |
| **Rental income** | Yes — taxable in India | Yes — worldwide income | Foreign tax credit in Canada |
| **Capital gains (property)** | Yes — taxable in India | Yes — worldwide income | Foreign tax credit in Canada |
| **Capital gains (shares)** | Yes — taxable in India | Yes — worldwide income | Foreign tax credit in Canada |
| **Salary (Indian employer)** | Yes — TDS applicable | Yes — if Canadian tax resident | Foreign tax credit in the country of residence |

### How to Claim DTAA Benefits

1. **Obtain a Tax Residency Certificate (TRC)** from CRA — this proves you are a Canadian tax resident
2. **Submit the TRC to your Indian bank** along with Form 10F to reduce TDS on NRO interest from 30% to 15%
3. **File Form 67** with the Indian Income Tax Department before or with your ITR
4. **Claim foreign tax credit in Canada** using Form T2209 when filing your Canadian T1 return for taxes already paid in India

### The Form 67 Trap

The Income Tax Department has strict rules about Form 67. It must be filed before or along with the ITR — not after. If you file your ITR first and then try to submit Form 67, the system may reject it. ## Penalties for Missing the ITR Deadline

Filing late is not just an inconvenience — it costs real money.

| Scenario | Penalty / Consequence |
|---|---|
| **Filing after July 31 but before December 31** | Late fee of ₹5,000 under Section 234F (₹1,000 if total income is below ₹5 lakh) |
| **Not filing at all** | Late fee + interest under Sections 234A/234B/234C + potential prosecution for chronic non-filers |
| **Interest on unpaid tax** | Section 234A: 1% per month (or part) on unpaid tax from the due date. Section 234B: 1% per month on shortfall in advance tax. Section 234C: interest for deferment of advance tax installments. |
| **Loss of carry-forward** | If you have capital losses, you cannot carry them forward to offset future gains if you file after July 31 |
| **Delayed refund** | Late filers go to the back of the processing queue — expect months of additional delay |

The late fee of ₹5,000 is the minimum cost. When you add interest on unpaid tax and the loss of carry-forward benefits, filing late can easily cost ₹10,000–₹50,000 or more depending on your income.

## Common Mistakes That Cost Canadian NRIs Money

### 1. Using ITR-1 Instead of ITR-2

NRIs cannot use ITR-1. Full stop. It does not matter how simple your return is — ITR-1 is exclusively for resident individuals. If you file using ITR-1, the department will issue a defective return notice, and you will need to refile correctly within 15 days.

### 2. Forgetting Form 67 for DTAA

Without Form 67, your DTAA claim is invalid. This means you lose the treaty benefit and pay the full Indian tax rate on your Indian income, even though you are also paying tax on the same income in Canada. For NRIs with significant NRO FD balances, this single oversight can cost thousands of rupees.

### 3. Not Reconciling TDS with Form 26AS

Banks and tenants sometimes deduct TDS but fail to deposit it with the government on time, or report it with errors. If the TDS shown in your ITR does not match Form 26AS, your refund will be held up. Always download Form 26AS and AIS before filing and verify every entry.

### 4. Incorrectly Declaring Residential Status

The portal asks you to self-declare whether you are a Non-Resident, Resident, or RNOR. If you select the wrong status, the system calculates your tax liability on the wrong set of income. For example, selecting "Resident" means your Canadian income also becomes taxable in India. This is a catastrophic error.

### 5. Not Claiming TDS Refunds

Banks deduct TDS at 30% on NRO FD interest. If your total Indian income is below ₹5 lakh, or if you are claiming DTAA benefits that reduce your effective rate, you may be entitled to a refund of excess TDS. But you only get the refund if you file an ITR claiming it.

### 6. Missing Advance Tax Obligations

If your Indian tax liability after TDS exceeds ₹10,000 in a financial year, you are technically required to pay advance tax in quarterly installments. Missing advance tax payments triggers interest under Sections 234B and 234C, even if you file your ITR on time. This is commonly relevant for NRIs with large capital gains from property sales.

### 7. Not Reporting All Income Sources

The Annual Information Statement (AIS) now captures a wide range of financial transactions — bank interest, dividend income, mutual fund sales, property transactions. If you omit income that appears in your AIS, the system will flag a mismatch, which can lead to a notice or scrutiny.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can NRIs file ITR after the July 31 deadline?

Yes. You can file a belated return until December 31, 2026. However, you will face a late fee of ₹5,000 under Section 234F (₹1,000 if your total income is below ₹5 lakh), interest on any unpaid tax under Section 234A at 1% per month, and you will lose the ability to carry forward capital losses. Filing on time is always the better option.

### Do NRIs in Canada need to file ITR if their only income is NRE FD interest?

No. NRE fixed deposit interest is fully tax-exempt in India, so it does not count toward your Indian income. If this is your only Indian income and no TDS was deducted on any other income, you are not required to file. However, you must still report this interest on your Canadian T1 return since Canada taxes worldwide income.

### Which ITR form should NRIs use — ITR-1 or ITR-2?

NRIs must use ITR-2 (or ITR-3 if they have business income). NRIs cannot use ITR-1 under any circumstances. ITR-1 is exclusively for resident individuals with total income up to ₹50 lakh from salary, one house property, and other sources. Filing with ITR-1 as an NRI will result in a defective return notice.

### How can Canadian NRIs claim DTAA benefits when filing ITR?

To claim DTAA benefits, you need three things: a Tax Residency Certificate from CRA proving you are a Canadian tax resident, Form 10F (a self-declaration), and Form 67 filed with the Income Tax Department before or along with your ITR. The DTAA allows you to reduce TDS on NRO interest from 30% to 15% and claim foreign tax credits in Canada for taxes paid in India. Services like ClearTax and Tax2Win handle Form 67 filing as part of their NRI plans.

### What is the penalty for not filing ITR as an NRI?

If your Indian income exceeds ₹2.5 lakh and you do not file, you face a late fee of up to ₹5,000 under Section 234F, interest on unpaid tax at 1% per month under Sections 234A/234B/234C, and you cannot claim TDS refunds or carry forward capital losses. In extreme cases of chronic non-filing, the department can initiate prosecution proceedings. For NRIs who sold property, not filing means the 20%+ TDS deducted by the buyer stays with the government even if your actual tax liability is lower.

### Can NRIs file ITR online from Canada?

Yes. The Income Tax Department's e-filing portal at incometax.gov.in is fully accessible from Canada. You can register, file, and e-verify your return entirely online. For e-verification, net banking through your Indian bank is the most reliable method for NRIs abroad. Alternatively, you can use a Digital Signature Certificate or send the signed ITR-V by post to CPC Bangalore.

### How long does it take to receive a refund after filing ITR?

Refund processing times vary, but if you file early (May–June) and e-verify immediately, you can typically expect your refund within 30–60 days. Filing during the peak rush in late July or filing a belated return pushes processing times to 3–6 months or longer. Ensure your bank account details (IFSC code, account number) are correctly linked to your PAN on the Income Tax portal to avoid refund failures.
