## Two Tax Systems, One Financial Year That Doesn't Align

US-based NRIs filing Indian taxes face a coordination problem that Canadian or UK NRIs don't: the US tax year is January through December, while India's financial year runs April through March. The income, the timelines, and the forms don't neatly line up. Add FBAR, FATCA, and the India-US DTAA's saving clause, and you have a compliance puzzle that's genuinely worth thinking through carefully.

This guide covers the 2026 Indian ITR filing season — Assessment Year 2026-27 (Financial Year 2025-26) — with specific attention to how it interacts with your US tax obligations. Key dates, the right forms, DTAA mechanics, and the coordination traps that cost US NRIs money every year.

## Key ITR Dates for 2026

| Deadline | Date | What It Means |
|---|---|---|
| **End of Financial Year** | March 31, 2026 | Last day of FY 2025-26. All Indian income through this date falls in AY 2026-27. Also the deadline for tax-saving investments under Sections 80C, 80D, etc. |
| **ITR Filing Deadline** | July 31, 2026 | File your ITR for FY 2025-26 by this date to avoid late fees. This is the date that matters for most NRIs. |
| **Belated Return Deadline** | December 31, 2026 | If you miss July 31, you can still file — with a late fee of up to ₹5,000 under Section 234F, plus interest on any unpaid tax. |
| **Revised Return Deadline** | December 31, 2026 | Filed on time but made errors? File a revised return by this date. |
| **Tax Audit Deadline** | October 31, 2026 | Relevant only if you have Indian business income requiring a tax audit. Most salaried US NRIs can ignore this. |
| **Advance Tax Installments** | June 15, Sept 15, Dec 15, Mar 15 | If your Indian tax liability after TDS exceeds ₹10,000, quarterly advance tax installments may be required. |
| **FBAR Deadline** | April 15, 2026 (auto-extended to Oct 15) | US FinCEN Form 114 for foreign accounts exceeding $10,000 aggregate. Filed separately from your 1040. |
| **US Federal Tax Deadline** | April 15, 2026 | Your Form 1040 is due. US citizens and residents abroad get an automatic 2-month extension to June 15. |

**The practical sequence for most US NRIs:** File your 1040 by April 15 (or June 15 with the abroad extension), file FBAR by October 15, and file your Indian ITR by July 31. The three deadlines don't conflict, but the documentation for each overlaps.

## Do You Actually Need to File an Indian ITR?

Not every US NRI is required to file. The threshold analysis:

### You Must File If:

- Your total **Indian income exceeds ₹2.5 lakh** for FY 2025-26 — this includes NRO FD interest, rental income, capital gains from shares or property sold in India, dividends, or salary from an Indian employer
- **TDS was deducted** on your Indian income and you want to claim a **refund** — banks deduct TDS at 30% on NRO FD interest, which may exceed your actual Indian tax liability if your total income is moderate
- You **sold property, mutual funds, or shares** in India during FY 2025-26 and have capital gains to report
- You are transitioning from NRI to RNOR (Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident) status

### You Are Not Required to File If:

- Your only Indian income is **NRE FD interest**, which is fully exempt in India
- Your total Indian income is **below ₹2.5 lakh** and no TDS was deducted

### Why File Even When Not Required

A filed return creates a compliance record with the Income Tax Department. If you plan to return to India, buy property, or apply for an Indian home loan, a history of filed returns — even nil returns — simplifies that process. It also starts the clock on the assessment window. File when in doubt.

## Which ITR Form to Use

Filing with the wrong form generates a defective return notice under Section 139(9), requiring a refile within 15 days. NRIs have a limited set of eligible forms:

| ITR Form | Use For | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **ITR-1 (Sahaj)** | Resident individuals only | **NRIs cannot use ITR-1 under any circumstances** |
| **ITR-2** | NRIs with salary, house property, capital gains, FD interest, dividends | **Correct form for the vast majority of US NRIs** |
| **ITR-3** | NRIs with business or professional income in India | Use if you have Indian business/freelance income |
| **ITR-4 (Sugam)** | Residents under presumptive taxation | **Not available to NRIs** |

**ITR-2 is the right form for most US NRIs.** It handles the full range of NRI income scenarios: rental income, NRO interest, capital gains from Indian stocks and property, dividends, and salary from Indian employers.

## Step-by-Step: Filing Your NRI ITR for AY 2026-27

### Step 1: Gather Documents

**Identity and Residency:**
- PAN card (mandatory)
- Valid Indian passport
- US visa, green card, or naturalization certificate
- Travel records — exact India entry and exit dates for FY 2025-26

**Income Documents:**
- Form 16 (if you had salary from an Indian employer)
- Bank statements for all NRE and NRO accounts (April 2025 – March 2026)
- TDS certificates (Form 16A) from banks, tenants, mutual fund houses
- Capital gains statements from Indian brokers and fund houses
- Rental income receipts and payment records

**For DTAA Claims:**
- IRS Form 6166 (US Tax Residency Certificate / Certificate of Coverage) — request from IRS
- Form 10F (self-declaration for DTAA claim — filed with Indian bank/department)
- Copy of your most recent US 1040 and W-2s (useful supporting documentation)

### Step 2: Determine Your Indian Residential Status

Your residential status under the Indian Income Tax Act determines which income India can tax.

For most US-based NRIs, this is straightforward: if you were in India for **fewer than 182 days** during FY 2025-26, you are an NRI for Indian tax purposes.

The more complex case is RNOR (Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident) — a transitional status that applies if you recently moved back to India, or under the expanded NRI rule if you have substantial Indian income (above ₹15 lakh) as an Indian citizen. RNOR determination involves the prior-year residency history and is best confirmed by a CA.

### Step 3: Log In to the Income Tax Portal

Go to [incometax.gov.in](https://www.incometax.gov.in) and log in with your PAN. If you're registering for the first time, you'll need your PAN, Aadhaar (if linked to PAN), and a working email and phone number. US phone numbers work for OTP verification.

### Step 4: Download Form 26AS and AIS

**Form 26AS** is your tax credit statement — it shows TDS deducted by banks, employers, and property buyers. **Annual Information Statement (AIS)** captures a broader set of financial transactions: mutual fund purchases, property transactions, dividend credits.

Cross-check both against your own records before filing. If there are discrepancies — TDS shown in your records that doesn't appear in 26AS — follow up with the deductor (usually your bank) before the filing deadline. Mismatches hold up refunds.

### Step 5: File Form 67 for DTAA Benefits

This is the step most US NRIs miss, and it costs them the treaty benefit entirely.

If you're claiming relief under the India-US DTAA — for example, a foreign tax credit in India for US taxes paid on Indian income, or reduced TDS rates on Indian interest — **you must file Form 67 before or alongside your ITR**. Form 67 requires you to upload your US Tax Residency Certificate (Form 6166) and declare the details of foreign income and taxes paid.

Without Form 67, the DTAA claim is invalid. Services like <AffiliateLink partner="cleartax">ClearTax</AffiliateLink> and <AffiliateLink partner="tax2win">Tax2Win</AffiliateLink> include Form 67 filing in their NRI plans, which is one of the strongest reasons to use a professional service.

### Step 6: Fill in ITR-2

Select ITR-2. Key schedules:

- **Personal Information** — Select "Non-Resident" as your residential status
- **Schedule HP** — Rental income (minus 30% standard deduction, minus home loan interest)
- **Schedule CG** — Capital gains from property, shares, mutual funds
- **Schedule OS** — NRO FD interest and other income from other sources
- **Schedule FSI** — Foreign source income (if claiming DTAA relief)
- **Schedule TR** — Tax relief under DTAA
- **Schedule FA** — Foreign assets (primarily for RNOR, but review if applicable)

### Step 7: Verify the Tax Computation and Submit

Review the auto-calculated liability against your own estimate. Confirm all TDS credits from Form 26AS appear. Submit the return.

### Step 8: E-Verify Within 30 Days

E-verification options for US residents:
- **Net banking** through your Indian bank's internet banking portal (most reliable for NRIs abroad)
- **Aadhaar OTP** — only if your Aadhaar is linked to PAN and your Indian mobile number is active
- **Digital Signature Certificate (DSC)**
- **Physical ITR-V by post** to CPC Bangalore (slowest — only if other options fail)

Net banking is the recommended path for US-based filers.

## India-US DTAA: How to Avoid Paying Tax Twice

The India-US Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement was signed in 1989 and last amended by protocol in 2000. It remains the operative treaty. Key income categories:

| Income Type | Taxable in India? | Taxable in USA? | DTAA Relief |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NRO FD interest** | Yes — 30% TDS (15% with DTAA, Article 11) | Yes — ordinary income | Foreign tax credit (Form 1116) in USA for Indian TDS paid |
| **NRE FD interest** | No — fully exempt | Yes — ordinary income (saving clause) | No double tax, but US taxes it fully; no credit available |
| **Rental income** | Yes — taxable in India | Yes — worldwide income | Foreign tax credit in USA |
| **Capital gains — property** | Yes — taxable in India | Yes — worldwide income | Foreign tax credit in USA |
| **Capital gains — shares/MFs** | Yes — taxable in India | Yes — PFIC rules may apply | Foreign tax credit + PFIC compliance |
| **Salary from Indian employer** | Yes — TDS | Yes — worldwide income | Foreign tax credit; possibly foreign earned income exclusion (Form 2555) |

### The Saving Clause: Why the DTAA Doesn't Protect US Citizens the Way It Protects Others

Article 1, Paragraph 4 of the India-US DTAA contains a saving clause: the US reserves the right to tax its citizens and residents as if the treaty did not exist. This means:

- If you are a US citizen or green card holder, the US taxes your worldwide income regardless of DTAA provisions
- You cannot use the treaty to eliminate US taxation on Indian-source income
- The treaty's value for US-based NRIs is mainly in reducing Indian-side TDS rates and resolving source-of-income questions

The Canada and UK treaties have similar clauses, but the US saving clause is among the broadest in scope.

### Form 1116: Foreign Tax Credit

For most income types where both countries tax the same income (NRO interest, rental income, capital gains), relief comes through **Form 1116** on your US 1040. You claim a credit for the Indian taxes paid — the credit reduces your US tax liability dollar-for-dollar, subject to limitation calculations. You pay the higher of the two countries' effective rates, not both.

### Form 2555: Foreign Earned Income Exclusion

If you have **salary income from an Indian employer** and meet the physical presence or bona fide residence test, you may be eligible to exclude a portion of that Indian salary from US taxation using **Form 2555**. The foreign earned income exclusion for 2025 (US tax year) is $126,500. You cannot claim both Form 2555 exclusion and Form 1116 credit on the same income — elect whichever produces a better outcome for your situation.

### Claiming DTAA Benefits in India

To get the reduced TDS rate on NRO interest:

1. Obtain **IRS Form 6166** (US Tax Residency Certificate) — request from the IRS International Accounts office. Allow 4–6 weeks.
2. Complete **Form 10F** — available on the Indian Income Tax portal
3. Submit both documents to your Indian bank before the next TDS deduction cycle
4. **File Form 67** with the Income Tax Department before or alongside your ITR

Without this documentation in place, your bank deducts TDS at 30%. With it, the rate drops to 15% under Article 11 of the treaty. On a ₹5 lakh NRO FD earning 7% annually, that's the difference between ₹10,500 deducted and ₹21,000 deducted.

## The US-India Calendar Year Mismatch

The US tax year is January 1 – December 31. India's financial year is April 1 – March 31. This creates a practical coordination issue:

**Example:** You sell Indian property in November 2025.
- India: The sale falls in FY 2025-26 (April 2025 – March 2026). You report it on your Indian ITR due July 31, 2026.
- USA: The sale falls in US tax year 2025 (January 2025 – December 2025). You report it on your Form 1040 due April 15, 2026.

You file the US return first, claim the capital gains there, then use the Indian taxes paid as a foreign tax credit. But the Indian ITR isn't filed until July 2026 — after your US return. In practice, you estimate Indian taxes paid (based on TDS deducted and advance tax paid) when filing the 1040, and amend if necessary. Keep documentation of all TDS deducted from Indian transactions.

**A cleaner approach:** Use a US CPA or dual-tax specialist who coordinates both returns. The savings from properly claiming foreign tax credits typically exceed the professional fees.

## State Tax Complications

Federal treatment of Indian income is consistent across all US states. State treatment is not.

- **California:** Does not recognize the foreign tax credit for state purposes. You pay California tax on Indian income with no offset for taxes paid to India. California also taxes income of California-domiciled taxpayers even while temporarily abroad.
- **New York:** Generally follows federal treatment for foreign tax credits, but has its own residency rules. NYC residents also pay a city income tax on worldwide income.
- **New Jersey:** Generally follows federal treatment but has some restrictions. New Jersey does not allow foreign earned income exclusion (Form 2555) at the state level.
- **Texas, Florida, Nevada:** No state income tax — simplifies the picture considerably.

If you live in a high-tax state like California or New York with significant Indian income, consult a CPA familiar with both state and India-US tax rules. The federal analysis alone is insufficient.

## Penalties for Missing the ITR Deadline

| Scenario | Cost |
|---|---|
| **Filing July 31 – December 31** | Late fee of ₹5,000 under Section 234F (₹1,000 if total income below ₹5 lakh) |
| **Not filing at all** | Late fee + Section 234A interest at 1%/month on unpaid tax + potential scrutiny |
| **Missing advance tax installments** | Interest under Sections 234B and 234C — 1%/month on shortfall |
| **Capital loss carry-forward lost** | Capital losses cannot be carried forward if filed after July 31 |
| **Refund delay** | Late filers go to the back of the processing queue — expect months of additional wait |

The ₹5,000 late fee is the floor. Add interest charges and the cost of losing loss carry-forwards, and late filing on a return with significant Indian income can easily cost ₹25,000–₹75,000 or more.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can NRIs in the US file their Indian ITR online?

Yes. The Income Tax Department's e-filing portal at incometax.gov.in is fully accessible from the US. You can register, file, and e-verify your return entirely online. For e-verification, net banking through an Indian bank is the most reliable method from abroad.

### Which ITR form should US-based NRIs use?

ITR-2 for most US NRIs. ITR-2 covers salary, house property income, capital gains, FD interest, dividends, and other sources. NRIs cannot use ITR-1 under any circumstances. ITR-3 applies only if you have Indian business or professional income.

### How does the India-US DTAA reduce TDS on NRO interest?

Article 11 of the India-US DTAA caps interest taxation at 15% for US residents, compared to the default 30% TDS. To claim this rate, submit an IRS Form 6166 (US Tax Residency Certificate) and Form 10F to your Indian bank before the TDS is deducted. Then file Form 67 with the Income Tax Department alongside your ITR to formally claim the DTAA benefit.

### Does selling Indian property affect my US taxes?

Yes. Capital gains from selling Indian property must be reported on your US 1040 in the year of the sale (US calendar year, not Indian financial year). You can claim a foreign tax credit (Form 1116) for Indian capital gains tax paid. The timing mismatch — India taxes the sale in one financial year, the US in a different calendar year — requires careful documentation.

### What is the FBAR deadline, and how does it relate to ITR filing?

FBAR (FinCEN Form 114) is due April 15 with an automatic extension to October 15. It is filed separately from your 1040 (through fincen.gov, not the IRS). The ITR deadline is July 31. The FBAR and ITR deadlines don't conflict — the FBAR reports account existence and maximum balances, while the ITR reports Indian income and claims DTAA benefits. Both filings are independent.

### Can NRIs claim foreign earned income exclusion (Form 2555) on Indian salary income?

Potentially, if you meet the physical presence test (330 days outside the US in a 12-month period) or bona fide residence test. However, Form 2555 and Form 1116 cannot be applied to the same income. For most US-based NRIs with Indian salary income from a prior Indian employer, the foreign tax credit (Form 1116) is more commonly applicable and may produce a better outcome than the exclusion. Get CPA advice before electing.

### What is the penalty for missing the July 31 Indian ITR deadline?

A late fee of ₹5,000 under Section 234F (₹1,000 if total income is below ₹5 lakh). On top of that, interest on unpaid tax accrues at 1% per month under Section 234A from the due date. If you had capital losses during the year, missing the deadline also forfeits your right to carry those losses forward to offset future gains — often the most expensive consequence for NRIs with active equity or property portfolios.

### Do I need to report Indian NRE FD interest on my US tax return?

Yes. While NRE FD interest is completely exempt from Indian tax, the IRS taxes it as ordinary foreign income. The India-US DTAA saving clause preserves the US's right to tax its residents on worldwide income. Report NRE interest on Schedule B of your Form 1040. No foreign tax credit is available since no Indian tax was paid on this income.
