## Introduction

Tax season in India runs from April through July, and if you are a Non-Resident Indian (NRI) with any financial ties to India, you cannot afford to ignore your Income Tax Return (ITR) filing obligations. Whether you earn rent from a flat in Mumbai, collect interest on your NRO fixed deposits, or sold an inherited property last year, the Indian Income Tax Department expects you to file — and file correctly.

The problem? NRI tax filing is meaningfully more complex than resident filing. You need to pick the right ITR form, claim benefits under the Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement (DTAA) — the treaty between India and your country of residence that prevents you from being taxed twice on the same income — and ensure your Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) credits are properly accounted for.

You broadly have three options:

1. **Online assisted platforms** like ClearTax and Tax2Win that pair you with a Chartered Accountant (CA)
2. **An independent CA** who specializes in NRI taxation
3. **DIY filing** on the Income Tax Department's own portal

This guide compares all three approaches with real pricing, honest pros and cons, and a clear recommendation based on your specific situation.

## Do You Even Need to File ITR as an NRI?

Before you spend money on a filing service, confirm whether you actually need to file. Here is a quick checklist.

**You must file an ITR if any of these apply:**

- Your total Indian income exceeds the basic exemption limit of **₹2.5 lakh** (for FY 2025-26)
- TDS was deducted on your Indian income (bank interest, rent, property sale) and you want a **refund**
- You sold property, shares, or mutual funds in India and need to report **capital gains**
- You hold Indian investments that generated taxable income
- You are transitioning from NRI to **Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR)** status

**You do not need to file if:**

- Your only Indian income is NRE fixed deposit interest (which is tax-exempt)
- Your total Indian income is below ₹2.5 lakh and no TDS was deducted

Even when filing is not mandatory, it is often smart to file a nil return to maintain a clean compliance record — especially if you plan to return to India or apply for a home loan.

Some CAs charge by the hour (₹2,000–₹5,000/hour) for advisory work on top of the filing fee.

### When to Hire an Independent CA

You should seriously consider an independent CA if:

- **You sold property in India** — NRI property sales involve TDS at 20%+, capital gains indexation, Section 54/54EC reinvestment exemptions, and repatriation certificates. Getting this wrong is expensive.
- **You have business or professional income in India** — This requires ITR-3 filing with proper P&L and balance sheet preparation.
- **You are transitioning from NRI to RNOR or Resident status** — The year of transition is notoriously complex. Your global income may partially become taxable in India, and the rules around RNOR are nuanced.
- **You have multiple income sources across countries** — If you earn income in India, the US, Canada, and the UK in the same year, a cookie-cutter platform approach may miss optimization opportunities.
- **You need ongoing tax planning advice** — Not just filing, but structural advice on NRE/NRO accounts, investment strategy, and repatriation planning.

## DIY Filing on the Income Tax Portal — Free But Risky

The Income Tax Department's e-filing portal at [incometax.gov.in](https://www.incometax.gov.in) allows anyone to file their return for free. For resident Indians with simple salaried income, it works well enough. For NRIs, it is a minefield.

### When DIY Filing Works

- Your only Indian income is NRO FD interest with TDS already deducted
- You are very comfortable with Indian tax forms and rules
- You understand which ITR form to use (ITR-2 for most NRIs, not ITR-1)
- Your return is genuinely simple with no DTAA claims

### Major Pitfalls for NRIs

- **Wrong ITR form** — NRIs cannot use ITR-1 (Sahaj). Using it anyway will result in a defective return notice. You need ITR-2 at minimum.
- **Missing DTAA benefits** — Claiming tax treaty relief requires filing Form 67 before the ITR due date and correctly entering treaty details. The portal does not guide you through this.
- **Incorrect residential status** — The portal asks you to self-declare your residential status. Getting this wrong cascades into incorrect tax calculations on every schedule.
- **TDS mismatch issues** — NRIs often have TDS deducted at higher rates (20% on FD interest vs. 10% for residents). Reconciling Form 26AS with your return requires care.
- **Foreign asset schedule** — If you hold assets abroad and your status requires disclosure (relevant for RNOR), the portal's Schedule FA is confusing to fill.

### Our Verdict

**We do not recommend DIY filing for most NRIs.** The cost of a professional service (₹5,000–₹10,000) is trivial compared to the potential cost of a wrong filing — lost DTAA benefits, incorrect TDS refund claims, or a defective return notice that delays your refund by months.

## Important Deadlines for 2026

Mark these dates. Missing them costs money.

- **July 31, 2026** — ITR filing deadline for FY 2025-26 (Assessment Year 2026-27). This is the most important date. Filing on time avoids a late fee of up to ₹5,000 under Section 234F.
- **December 31, 2026** — Last date to file a **revised return** if you made an error in your original filing.
- **December 31, 2026** — Last date to file a **belated return** if you missed the July 31 deadline. A late fee of ₹5,000 (₹1,000 if income is below ₹5 lakh) applies.
- **March 31, 2026** — Deadline for tax-saving investments under Section 80C, 80D, etc., for FY 2025-26. If you are reading this article on or after its publication date, this deadline has already passed.

**Pro tip:** File early — ideally in May or June. You avoid the July rush, get faster refunds, and the CAs on platforms like ClearTax and Tax2Win have more bandwidth to give your return proper attention.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can NRIs file their ITR themselves without a CA?

Yes, NRIs can file ITR themselves on the Income Tax Department's e-filing portal (incometax.gov.in) at no cost. However, we do not recommend it for most NRIs. The process involves selecting the correct ITR form (ITR-2 or ITR-3, never ITR-1), correctly declaring residential status, claiming DTAA benefits with Form 67, and reconciling TDS. Mistakes are common and can result in lost refunds, defective return notices, or missed tax treaty benefits. A professional CA-assisted service costing ₹5,000–₹10,000 is a worthwhile investment.

### Which ITR form should NRIs use?

Most NRIs should use ITR-2, which covers income from salary, house property, capital gains, and other sources (like bank interest). NRIs cannot use ITR-1 (Sahaj), which is exclusively for resident individuals. If you have business or professional income in India, you will need ITR-3. If you file using the wrong form, the Income Tax Department will issue a defective return notice under Section 139(9), and you will need to refile within 15 days.

### What happens if an NRI doesn't file ITR in India?

If your Indian income exceeds the basic exemption limit of ₹2.5 lakh and you do not file, you may face a late filing fee of up to ₹5,000 under Section 234F, interest on any unpaid taxes under Sections 234A/234B/234C, and potential scrutiny from the Income Tax Department. Additionally, you will not be able to claim TDS refunds or carry forward capital losses. For NRIs selling property, not filing means the buyer's TDS deduction (typically 20%+) stays with the government even if your actual tax liability is lower.

### Can NRIs claim DTAA benefits while filing ITR?

Yes, and you should. DTAA (Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement) benefits allow NRIs to avoid paying tax twice on the same income — once in India and once in their country of residence. To claim these benefits, you must file Form 67 before or along with your ITR, and you need a Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) from your country of residence. Services like ClearTax and Tax2Win handle Form 67 filing as part of their NRI plans. Without DTAA claims, you may end up paying significantly more tax than necessary.

### How long does NRI tax filing take with an online service?

With ClearTax, expect 3–5 business days from the time you submit all documents. Tax2Win typically takes 4–7 business days. During peak season (late June through July), turnaround times can stretch by a few additional days. Filing early in the season (April–May) gets you faster processing and more attention from the assigned CA. An independent CA may take 5–15 business days depending on the complexity of your return and their workload.
