## How We Calculated the True Cost

Most people compare money transfer services by looking at the upfront fee. That's a mistake. The fee is often the smaller part of what you pay.

**The true cost of any international transfer = Transfer Fee + Exchange Rate Markup.**

Here's what we mean. The mid-market rate (also called the interbank rate) is the real exchange rate — the one you see on Google or XE.com. As of mid-March 2026, that's approximately 1 CAD = 67.46 INR.

When your bank offers you a rate of, say, 1 CAD = 64.50 INR instead of 67.46 INR, they're pocketing roughly 4.4% on the exchange rate — or about CAD 44 on every CAD 1,000 you send. They do this on top of the CAD 25–50 wire transfer fee they already charged you.

**The exchange rate markup is where banks make their real money on international transfers.** Throughout this guide, we calculate the total cost by adding the transfer fee to the estimated exchange rate markup, so you can see exactly what each service actually costs.

## NRE vs NRO: Which Account Should You Send To?

If you're sending money to your own accounts in India, understanding the difference between NRE and NRO accounts is critical:

**NRE (Non-Resident External) Account:**
- Designed for parking your foreign earnings in India
- Funds are **fully repatriable** (you can send money back to Canada freely)
- Interest earned is **tax-free in India**
- Maintained in INR, but funded from foreign currency

**NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary) Account:**
- Designed for managing India-sourced income (rental income, pensions, dividends)
- Repatriation is **limited to USD 1 million per financial year** (with documentation)
- Interest earned is **taxable in India**
- Can receive both foreign and domestic deposits

**For most remittances from Canada, you should send to your NRE account.** This ensures the money remains freely repatriable and the interest is tax-free. Use your NRO account only for India-sourced income.

## Tax Implications of Sending Money from Canada to India

One of the most common worries NRIs have: "Will I be taxed on money I send to India?" Here's the straightforward answer:

### No Tax on Sending Money

**Money you send from Canada to India is not taxable in either country.** You're transferring your own after-tax income. There's no gift tax in Canada, and India does not tax inbound remittances from NRIs (though the recipient's tax situation may differ if they aren't a close relative — check India's gift tax rules under Section 56(2) of the Income Tax Act).

### FINTRAC Reporting (CAD 10,000+)

Under Canadian law, financial institutions must report international electronic funds transfers of **CAD 10,000 or more** to FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada). This is an automatic reporting requirement — you don't need to do anything, and it doesn't mean you're being investigated. It's routine anti-money-laundering compliance.

### T1135 Foreign Income Verification (CAD 100,000+ in Foreign Assets)

If the total cost of your foreign property (including Indian bank accounts, real estate, and investments) exceeds **CAD 100,000 at any point during the year**, you must file Form T1135 with your Canadian tax return. This is a reporting requirement, not a tax — but failing to file carries significant penalties.

### Key Takeaway

**Sending money isn't taxed, but large amounts trigger reporting requirements.** Keep records of all transfers, and if your Indian assets exceed CAD 100,000, make sure your accountant files T1135 with your annual return.
